# SeatSense — Proposal (Problem 2: Crowdedness-aware journey planner)

## The problem
A Delhi commuter can find out which bus to take, but not whether they will get on it, stand for 40 minutes, or watch it pass full. Journey planners rank by time only. Operators know buses are crowded, but not which stretch, which half-hour, or where riders are left behind.

## What we built
A working planner and operator tool on top of 2.56 million October 2026 e-tickets:

1. **Crowding engine.** On-board load for every segment of 1,238 route-directions, every 30 minutes, weekday and weekend, with a low–high range and a confidence grade.
2. **Commuter planner.** From / to / time → direct, one-change and two-change options (with short walks), each with total time, crowding level, the most crowded stretch, and % of the ride spent standing. A slider trades time for comfort. A **Best time to leave** chart shows trip time and crowding for departures from 30 minutes earlier to 90 minutes later, and a tip names the calmest one (e.g. Shakarpur → Kendriya Terminal: standing at 08:30; leave at 09:00 for a seat, and arrive 3 minutes sooner). Badarpur → Saket shows the trade-off directly: the 717A takes 39 min packed, route 34 takes 46 min with seats.
3. **Operator view.** The routes where demand exceeds what one bus can carry (riders left behind; 164 routes at weekday 08:30), each at its worst stretch, on a citywide map, and a stop × time heatmap plus crowding map for any route.
4. **Map, walks and changes.** Every option is drawn on a map stretch by stretch in its crowding colour; trips can include a 400 m walk and up to two changes.

## Why it is different
- **Honest about the sample.** E-tickets are only ~3–4 % of tickets, and women (free pink pass) and pass holders never appear. We scale with an explicit range and cap it by bus capacity per route, instead of presenting raw counts as reality.
- **Needs no extra data.** Stop order (median 100 % passenger-consistent) and travel times (from successive tickets of the same bus) are inferred from the ticket data alone.
- **Time-aware along the ride.** Crowding is read for each stretch at the half-hour the bus reaches it.
- **Built for both audiences** the brief names: commuters and operators.

## Evidence it works
- Week-to-week correlation of route × half-hour volume: 0.93–0.94.
- Weekday peaks at 8–10 am and 5–8 pm, quieter weekends; loads build towards metro hubs in the morning (e.g. 717A towards Saket Metro).
- Ticket-derived bus speeds: 1.4 min/stop at dawn, 2.5 min/stop at the 6–7 pm peak.
- At weekday peaks, weighted by riders: 56 % seats likely · 10 % few seats · 23 % standing · 11 % packed, so about one rider in three stands; 5,936 stretch × half-hours need more than one bus.
- Checked against independent sources: the GTFS timetable (trip times 0.93×, buses on the road 0.61×) and published totals: our range gives 22.2–40.0 lakh riders a day, and the Economic Survey of Delhi reports 24.29 lakh (DTC 2025-26) and 40.8 lakh (DTC + cluster FY2024-25); we see 6,023 buses a weekday against a published fleet of 6,100–6,300.
- 6,724 stop spellings cleaned into 4,959 names, then split by place where one name is two different stops (12,696 stop places), so a change of bus never jumps across the city; 93 % of route stops placed on the map by aligning each route with the public timetable.
- Weighted by riders, 95 % of crowding answers are medium or high confidence; a split-half test (odd vs even days) confirms higher grades agree more.

## Built to be checked
Every trip and tab has a shareable link; 18 automated API tests; the app runs in ~160 MB and deploys with one Dockerfile; every number above is regenerated by the pipeline and shown in the app's **How we know** tab.
